Can we trust the recalled before-ratings?

Participants rated their "before" knowledge after the training, which can inflate gains. Three checks: the gain under different data-quality rules (chart), whether gains follow what the training teaches, and whether people reported "no change" freely.

Statistical tests

Computed live from the survey data. p-values are adjusted for running 16 tests (Holm). Effect sizes say how big a difference is; p-values only say whether it could be chance. Responses from the same session share a presenter, so p-values are somewhat optimistic; the session-level check (sessions counted once) guards against that.

How the numbers are built

Knowledge items. Each person rates three statements after the training and again "thinking back to before": I can identify mental health challenges and crises; I can describe 988 and how it works; I know when to use 988. Answers score 1 (Strongly disagree) to 5 (Strongly agree). Gain is after minus before.

Recommend score (NPS). Percent answering 9–10 minus percent answering 0–6 on "How likely are you to recommend the training?" Ranges from −100 to +100.

Very likely to call. Share answering 9–10 on "How likely are you to call 988?" Only some form versions asked it.

Confidence intervals. Wilson intervals for shares, t-intervals for averages, and the standard interval for NPS. Before/after tests are Wilcoxon signed-rank tests; group comparisons use Mann-Whitney, Kruskal-Wallis, two-proportion and Fisher's exact tests.

Sessions. The form has no session or presenter field, so a session is the training date plus county.

Left out. Responses with "Strongly disagree" on every after-item but a recommend score of 8+ or positive presenter ratings are treated as a misread scale. Everything else is kept and flagged.

Privacy. Groups and sessions under responses show counts only.

Limits to keep in mind

Before-ratings are recalled after training; intent to call is not the same as calling, and there is no follow-up.

Most responses are students in two counties. With no age, grade, race or income fields, equity comparisons stop at role, county and language.

Spanish speakers are found from the form language and the language of their comments, so people who wrote no comment are missed.

Comment themes come from keyword rules. They are directional and should be spot-checked by a person.

About this version

This page was rebuilt from EveryMind988_Cleaned.xlsx, which lacked four columns the original dashboard used. Spanish speakers combine the form language with comments written in Spanish. "Same answer on every rating" means all six knowledge ratings and any presenter ratings match. "Needed a correction" counts only filled-in training dates, because the file doesn't mark which rows had answer labels recoded. Comment themes were recoded with keyword rules to the original dashboard's categories, so shares can differ slightly from the original.

How AI was used

Claude helped write the cleaning rules, statistics and this page. In this version, the group figures were checked against the workbook's summary sheets.
